Privacy Policy: kroma order extension

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Applies to: the kroma order extension for Google Chrome ("the extension"). The kroma app and this website have their own policy.

The extension finds the clothes in your order emails and makes a file you can import into the kroma app. The short version: your emails are read inside your own browser. They are not sent to us, to an AI service, or to anyone else. We don't run a server, so there is nowhere for them to go.

This page sets out exactly what the extension reads, what it sends and to whom, and what it keeps.

Who we are

kroma is operated by Pomp Clothing, United Kingdom. You can contact us at hello@kroma.fit.

We receive none of the data the extension handles, so for that data we are not a data controller under UK GDPR or EU GDPR. If you email us, we are the controller for that email; the main privacy policy covers it.

What the extension reads

When you click Connect Gmail and scan, you sign in with Google and grant the extension read-only access to your Gmail (gmail.readonly). It then:

  • Searches your mail for orders. It asks Gmail for messages in Gmail's "Purchases" category, or whose subject contains words such as order, receipt, purchase, invoice, refund or return, within the period you chose. Mail in Spam and Bin is not searched.
  • Reads those messages (sender, subject, date and content) to find the items you bought: name, size, colour, price, quantity, order number, order date, the shop's name, and the picture and link for each item.
  • Reads the address of the Gmail account you connected, only so that the "Open in Gmail" links on the results page open the right account.

The extension cannot send, delete, move, label or change any email, or mark one as read. Gmail does not offer a narrower permission that covers only receipts, so read-only access to your mail is the least the extension can ask for. It does not read your contacts, calendar, files or any other Google data.

Where your email is processed

Everything is worked out by code running in the extension's page in your browser. Email content is held in your browser's memory while the page is open and is gone when you close the tab.

  • Email content is not sent to kroma. We have no server to receive it.
  • Email content is not sent to any AI or machine-learning service, and is not used to train or improve any AI model.
  • No person at kroma can read your email or the list of what you bought.
  • The extension has no analytics, no advertising, no trackers and no accounts.

What the extension sends, and to whom

The extension contacts three kinds of service. We list them exhaustively.

1. Google

To sign you in and to read your mail through the Gmail API. This is a direct connection between your browser and Google, under Google's privacy policy. As soon as a scan has read your emails, the extension gives up its access and asks Google to revoke it.

2. The shops named in your order emails

To get a picture of each item you tick, the extension may request, from the shop's own website or picture server:

  • the product picture whose address is in the email;
  • on some shops, a search of the shop's site for the product's name, to find the picture the shop shows today.

These requests carry the picture's address or the product's name. They do not carry your email, your name or the rest of your order, and they are sent without your cookies for that shop. Like any web request, they show the shop your IP address.

The extension never follows the links in your emails. Those links are usually tracking links that are personal to you, and opening one tells the shop the email was clicked. A product link is saved in your order file only when it already points straight at the shop's own website; tracking links are discarded.

Some shops serve the pictures themselves from an address that records when a picture is loaded. Fetching such a picture can tell the shop that the email was opened, in the same way as opening the email in Gmail with pictures switched on. If you would rather avoid that, decline the "read and change your data on all websites" request when you start a scan: the extension will then build your file without pictures and make none of the requests in sections 2 and 3.

3. The Internet Archive

If a shop has removed a picture, the extension asks the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (archive.org) whether it saved a copy. Those requests carry the address or file name of the product picture, or the address of the product page, and show the Internet Archive your IP address. They carry nothing else from your email. The Internet Archive's terms and privacy policy apply.

Requests of kinds 2 and 3 are made only after you allow them. Chrome asks for this when you start a scan, as permission to "read and change your data on all websites". The extension needs that broad wording because it cannot know in advance which shops are in your mail. It uses the permission only to make the requests described here. It does not read, record or change the web pages you visit, and it collects no browsing history.

What is stored

  • By the extension: nothing. It keeps no copy of your emails, your orders or your pictures after you close the tab.
  • Your Google sign-in is not kept. Access to your Gmail lasts for one scan. When the scan has read your emails, the extension drops its access and asks Google to revoke it, so you sign in again for each scan.
  • On your computer, when you choose to download it: the order file (kroma-orders-…json). It holds the items you ticked: shop, order number, order date, item name, type, colour, size, quantity, price, product link and picture. It stays in your Downloads folder until you move or delete it. You import it into kroma yourself; kroma reads it on your phone and sends it nowhere.
  • On your computer, only if you ask for it: a diagnostic report. It lists every order and item the scan found, how each was read, and the subject lines of order emails the extension could not read. It has no email content and no pictures. It is for troubleshooting, and it is sent to us only if you choose to attach it to an email.

Google's Limited Use requirements

The extension's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In plain terms, for the data the extension reads from your Gmail:

  • It is used only to give you the feature you asked for: listing the clothes in your order emails and making your order file.
  • It is not transferred to anyone, other than the limited product details sent to shops and the Internet Archive to fetch pictures, as described above.
  • It is not used for advertising, and is never sold.
  • It is not read by any person at kroma.
  • It is not used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.

Permissions

  • Sign in with Google (identity): to ask for your consent to read-only Gmail access.
  • gmail.googleapis.com: to read your mail through the Gmail API.
  • oauth2.googleapis.com: to ask Google to revoke the extension's access when a scan ends.
  • All websites (optional, asked when you scan): to fetch product pictures from the shops in your orders and from the Internet Archive. Nothing else.

The extension adds nothing to the web pages you visit, runs nothing in the background, and loads no code from the internet.

Security

  • There is no kroma server and no account. No copy of your emails or your orders exists anywhere but your own browser and computer, so there is no central store that could be broken into or leaked.
  • The extension answers to no one else. It adds nothing to the web pages you visit, and websites and other extensions cannot ask it for your data.
  • Email content is treated as untrusted. Emails are read as text; nothing inside an email is ever run. The extension loads no code from the internet.
  • Requests go only to public web addresses, over an encrypted connection (https). The extension refuses any address in an email that points at your own computer or a device on your home network.
  • Access to Gmail is read-only and lasts for one scan. It is revoked as soon as your emails have been read, so the extension holds no standing access to your mailbox between scans.
  • Your order file is an ordinary, unencrypted file. Anyone who can open it can see what you bought. Treat it like any personal document: delete it from your Downloads folder, and from wherever you used to move it to your phone, once it is imported.

No software can promise to be free of flaws. If you find a security problem, email hello@kroma.fit and we will act on it.

Removing access

You don't normally need to do anything: the extension removes its own access to your Gmail at the end of every scan, and the results page tells you that it has.

If a scan is cut short, for example because the tab was closed or the connection dropped, the extension tries to remove its access but may not manage to. You can always remove it yourself, in any of these ways:

  • Open the extension and click Disconnect Gmail, which appears at the top if any access is left.
  • Remove "kroma orders" at myaccount.google.com/connections.
  • Remove the extension from Chrome at chrome://extensions.

Because the extension stores nothing, there is nothing further to delete. Delete the order file from your Downloads folder when you no longer need it.

Children

The extension is not directed at children under 13 (or under 16, in jurisdictions where that is the applicable age of digital consent). We do not knowingly process personal data from children, and the extension sends us no data about anyone.

Your rights (UK GDPR / EU GDPR)

We hold no data about you from the extension, so there is nothing for us to give you access to, correct, export or erase. Your order file and any diagnostic report are on your own computer and under your control.

If you have emailed us, your rights over that correspondence are set out in the main privacy policy. You can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk) or your local EU data protection authority.

Changes to this policy

We'll update this page before any change to how the extension handles data takes effect, and change the "Last updated" date at the top. If the extension ever asks for more access than described here, it will ask you first.
