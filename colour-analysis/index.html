Colour analysis (also called seasonal colour analysis or personal colour analysis) matches clothes and makeup to the natural colouring of your skin, hair and eyes. Colours that share your colouring make your skin look clearer and your eyes brighter. Colours that fight it can make you look tired, even when the clothes fit perfectly.

The 12-season system reads three traits and names your season after the strongest one:

  • Undertone (temperature): does your colouring lean warm and golden, or cool and pink-blue?
  • Depth (value): is your overall colouring light, medium or deep?
  • Chroma (clarity): is it bright and clear, or soft and blended?

The quiz

Answer in natural daylight, without makeup if you can, and think about your natural hair colour.

1. Look at the veins on the inside of your wrist. What colour are they?
2. Which jewellery looks better against your skin?
3. Hold something crisp white, then something cream, under your chin. Which flatters you?
4. How would you describe your skin depth?
5. What is your natural hair colour?

If you're grey or colour your hair, pick the colour it was in your twenties.

6. Does your natural hair have a warm or cool cast?
7. How would you describe your eyes?
8. How much contrast is there between your hair, skin and eyes?
9. When people compliment what you're wearing, it's usually…
10. How do you look in black near your face?

Other colour systems

The 12-season system descends from Johannes Itten's colour theory and Carole Jackson's four seasons. It's the most common system in Western colour analysis, but it isn't the only way to think about colour. Kroma treats five systems as equals:

  • Ritu, drawn from the six seasons of the Indian calendar and the colours of Indian textiles and festivals. Colours for Indian and brown skin tones
  • Korean Tonal (퍼스널 컬러), which splits warm and cool into eight types and is huge in Korean beauty culture. Korean personal colour analysis
  • Shikisai (色彩), drawn from Japanese traditional colour and the kasane layering of kimono dress
  • Wu Xing (五行), the five Chinese elements of Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water, each with its own colours

Ritu, Shikisai and Wu Xing as personal colour systems are Kroma's own interpretations of these traditions, not established practices. We say so in the app too.

How to do a colour analysis at home

  1. Use daylight. Stand facing a window, out of direct sun. Indoor bulbs are usually warm and will push you towards a warm result.
  2. Take off makeup and pull your hair back if it's dyed.
  3. Drape and compare. Hold two colours under your chin, one at a time: silver against gold, white against cream, then a bright colour against a muted one. Look at your skin, not the fabric. The better colour evens out redness and shadows. The worse one makes them stand out.
  4. Check your wardrobe. The clothes you get compliments in usually share your traits.

Is a free colour analysis as good as a paid one?

A trained analyst draping fabric on you in good light is the gold standard, and if you can book one, it's a lovely experience. A quiz like this one points you to the right family of seasons. Read your result page and the close-second page, then check them against your own wardrobe.

What does "true winter" or "dark autumn" mean?

Different analysts use different names for the same 12 seasons. True = Warm or Cool, Clear = Bright, Dark = Deep. Kroma uses Light, Warm, Bright, Cool, Soft and Deep.

Do I have to stick to my palette?

No. Kroma gives every colour a way to wear it, with no "avoid" lists. Colours outside your palette work well away from your face: on trousers, shoes and bags.
