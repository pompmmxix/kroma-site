details:
- heading: Best hair colours for Bright Winter
  body: |
    Your natural hair is often dark brown or black, with clear contrast against your skin and eyes. Colour that keeps the contrast clean and cool suits you best.

    - **Flattering:** black, blue-black, cool dark brown, and crisp, defined cool highlights. On lighter Bright Winters, an icy platinum can look striking.
    - **Harder to wear:** golden brown, copper, caramel and soft, muddy balayage. They're too warm or too blended for vivid colouring.
- heading: Jewellery and metals
  body: |
    Bright, polished metals suit you: shiny silver, platinum and white gold. Bold, statement pieces look right on you, because your colouring is vivid.

    For stones, choose clear and saturated ones: diamonds, crystal, sapphire, ruby, emerald, tourmaline, onyx and bright white pearls.
- heading: Denim, prints and fabrics
  body: |
    - **Denim:** dark rinse, black and crisp white denim. Faded, dusty washes look tired.
    - **Prints:** bold and graphic: colour-blocking, black-and-white geometrics, bright stripes and vivid florals on black or white.
    - **Fabrics:** crisp, smooth and shiny: poplin, satin, silk, patent leather and sequins.
- heading: Colours Bright Winter should avoid
  body: |
    The least flattering colours are muted, warm or earthy: the opposite of bright and cool.

    - **Dusty, greyed colours** like sage, mauve and dusty rose
    - **Earthy colours** like khaki, rust, olive and mustard
    - **Beige**, **camel** and **taupe** near your face
    - **Warm, muted pastels** like peach and apricot

    Pair muted pieces with a vivid jewel tone or black and white near your face.
- heading: Bright Winter vs Bright Spring
  body: |
    Both are bright first, so both suit vivid, saturated colours and high contrast. The difference is temperature.

    | | Bright Winter | Bright Spring |
    |---|---|---|
    | Undertone | Cool-neutral | Warm-neutral |
    | Best reds | Cherry, true red, blue-red | Tomato, poppy, coral-red |
    | Best pinks | Hot pink, fuchsia | Hot coral, warm watermelon |
    | Best greens | Emerald, bright jade | Kelly green, bright turquoise |
    | Best white | Pure white | Bright cream, warm white |

    A quick test: hold fuchsia and coral under your face in daylight. If fuchsia brightens your skin and coral looks orange, you're more likely Bright Winter.
- heading: Bright Winter vs Cool Winter and Deep Winter
  body: |
    All three Winters suit black, white and jewel tones. **Cool Winter** is the iciest and most blue-based. **Deep Winter** is the darkest: pine, aubergine and oxblood. Bright Winter is the most vivid and saturated of the three, with electric colours like hot pink, electric blue and emerald.
faq:
- q: Is Clear Winter the same as Bright Winter?
  a: >-
    Yes. Different colour analysis systems use different names for the same season. Clear Winter and Bright Winter both describe the most vivid Winter type.
- q: Can Bright Winter wear pastels?
  a: >-
    Icy pastels like icy pink, icy blue and icy lavender can work, especially paired with black or a vivid colour. Dusty or warm pastels look flat on you.
- q: Can Bright Winter wear gold?
  a: >-
    Silver, platinum and white gold usually look best. Because Bright Winter borders Bright Spring, a bright, polished gold can work for some, especially mixed with silver.
- q: Can Bright Winter wear brown?
  a: >-
    Brown is one of the harder neutrals for Bright Winter. If you want it, choose a very dark, cool brown, and pair it with a bright colour.
- q: What are the best neutrals for Bright Winter?
  a: >-
    Black, pure white, navy, charcoal and grey. Build a capsule wardrobe from these, then add power colours like true red, emerald, electric blue, hot pink and purple.

Bright Winter's leading trait is clarity, so muted, dusty colours dull it. Its second trait is coolness, which separates it from the warmer Bright Spring.

Bright Winters usually have striking contrast: dark hair with light or medium skin, or clear bright eyes against deeper skin. Eyes are often clear blue, bright green, or dark brown with a strong edge.
