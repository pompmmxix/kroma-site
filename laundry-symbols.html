The quick rules. A cross means "don't". More dots mean more heat. More bars under the wash tub mean a gentler cycle. A number in the tub is the maximum water temperature in °C.

Washing symbols

The tub means washing. The number is the maximum water temperature in °C; the bars underneath show how gentle the cycle should be.

SymbolMeaningWhat to do
Machine wash at 30°CCold to cool wash. Most everyday clothes, darks and colours.
Machine wash at 40°CWarm wash. Cottons, linen and everyday whites.
Machine wash at 60°CHot wash. Towels, bedding and sturdy whites.
30°C, gentle (synthetics) cycleOne bar under the tub means a gentler cycle with a slower spin.
30°C, very gentle (wool / delicates) cycleTwo bars mean the most gentle cycle: wool or delicates programme.
Hand wash onlyWash by hand in water no warmer than 40°C. Don't wring.
Wash at 30°C (US style)In the US system, dots show temperature: one dot is cold, up to six dots for 95°C.
Do not washUsually means dry clean only.

Bleaching symbols

The triangle covers bleach and some stain removers.

SymbolMeaningWhat to do
Any bleach allowedChlorine or oxygen bleach is fine.
Non-chlorine bleach onlyUse oxygen-based bleach or a colour-safe stain remover only.
Do not bleachNo bleach of any kind.

Drying symbols

A square means drying. A circle inside the square means a tumble dryer; lines inside mean natural drying.

SymbolMeaningWhat to do
Tumble dry, low heatOne dot: low temperature.
Tumble dry, normal heatTwo dots: normal temperature.
Do not tumble dryAir-dry instead.
Line dryHang on a line or airer.
Drip dryHang up soaking wet, without wringing or spinning.
Dry flatLay flat on a towel to keep the shape. Common on knitwear.
Dry in the shadeTwo lines in the corner: keep out of direct sun to stop fading. Combines with the symbols above.

Ironing symbols

The iron's dots show the maximum soleplate temperature.

SymbolMeaningWhat to do
Iron on low (up to 110°C)Synthetics like acrylic, nylon and acetate. Often no steam.
Iron on medium (up to 150°C)Wool, silk and polyester.
Iron on high (up to 200°C)Cotton and linen.
Do not ironSteaming may still be fine; check the fabric.

Dry cleaning symbols

A circle is for professional cleaning. The letter tells the cleaner which process to use; you don't need to know the details.

SymbolMeaningWhat to do
Dry clean (P)Professional dry clean with the most common solvents.
Dry clean (F)Dry clean with gentler, hydrocarbon solvents only.
Professional wet clean (W)A professional water-based clean.
Do not dry clean

UK and US care labels

UK and European labels follow the GINETEX system (ISO 3758) and usually print the wash temperature in °C inside the tub. US labels follow ASTM D5489 and often use dots for wash temperature instead, from one dot (cold, about 30°C) up to six dots (about 95°C). The other symbols are the same. Some US labels also add text such as "wash warm" next to the symbols.

Common questions

What does the triangle on a clothing label mean?

The triangle is about bleach. An empty triangle allows any bleach, two diagonal lines mean non-chlorine bleach only, and a crossed-out triangle means no bleach.

What does a square with a circle in it mean?

That's tumble drying. The dots inside show the heat (one for low, two for normal), and a cross means don't tumble dry.

Can I machine wash something that says hand wash?

Often yes, on a cold hand-wash or wool programme in a mesh bag, but the label is the safe choice for wool, silk and anything with embellishment.

What if the label has been cut off?

Wash cool (30°C) on a gentle cycle, skip the tumble dryer, and iron on low. That's safe for almost everything. Kroma saves the care symbols when you scan a label, so you don't lose them.
