What it does

Find your colours

Take a selfie and AI reads your colouring in five systems: 12-season Western, Ritu (South Asian), Shikisai (Japanese), Korean Tonal, and Wu Xing (Chinese). Plus Kibbe and proportion-based body analysis — pick the framework that fits you.

Know what suits you before you buy

Every garment you own, and everything on your wishlist, is scored against your palette. See what works with your colouring, spot near-duplicates of what you already have, and check the cost before you spend.

Outfits for the weather and your day

Daily suggestions from your own clothes, for everyday, work, smart or going out. Weather is manual chips by default — zero network — or opt in to Open-Meteo once a morning using a city you type. Personal comfort calibration so your "warm" isn't someone else's.

Your wardrobe, catalogued

Photograph each garment and AI tags type, colour, pattern, weave, and fabric where visible, across 120+ garment types from world dress traditions. Or start from what you've bought: the kroma order extension for Chrome finds the clothes in your order emails. Snap your outfit and kroma logs what you wore, for real cost-per-wear.

Bring your own AI

Use your own key for Gemini, Claude, OpenAI, Mistral or OpenRouter, or point kroma at an Ollama server you run yourself. Photos go straight from your phone to the provider you chose, and kroma asks before the first one is sent. We never see them.

No accounts, no servers

Open the app and start cataloguing — no sign-up, no email, no profile waiting on someone else's database. Your wardrobe lives in encrypted storage on your phone. There's no server we'd have to shut down to honour a deletion request, because there's no server.

Where your clothes actually come from

Most apps treat paisley as a Scottish pattern. It isn't.

The teardrop motif — boteh, ambi, kairi — comes from the Indian subcontinent, refined to its highest form in Mughal-era Kashmiri shawls. It got its English name because 19th-century Scottish mills mass-produced cheap copies. kroma tells that story, and dozens like it, inside the app.

Tap any pattern, colour, or fabric on a garment and the attribution opens up: where it actually originates, how it travelled, what its English name is really a record of. Indigo, Banarasi silk, Kuba cloth, Tatreez, Bandhani, Phulkari, Huipil, Mola, Kente, Aran, Sashiko — 74 textile traditions, each credited honestly.

If we got something wrong, especially about your community's tradition, there's a button inside every info modal that opens a pre-filled email straight to us. We'd rather hear from you and fix it than guess.

How the privacy piece actually works

kroma does not run a backend. Your wardrobe data lives on your phone. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising IDs, no crash reporters. The few places data can leave your device — the AI provider you chose, the weather service you opted in to, a product page you shared into the app — are listed exhaustively in the privacy policy.

That's not a marketing position. It's the architecture. There's no server we'd have to shut down to honour a deletion request, because there's no server.
