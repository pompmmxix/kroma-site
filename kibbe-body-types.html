The system comes from stylist David Kibbe's 1987 book Metamorphosis. It places you on a scale between yang (long, narrow, sharp, angular) and yin (soft, rounded, curved, delicate). Most people are a mix. Your type tells you which clothing lines echo your own.

How to find your Kibbe type

Look at your whole frame rather than one feature, and answer honestly. It helps to use photos taken straight on, full length, in fitted clothes.

  1. Vertical line: do you look tall and long (even if you're not), moderate, or compact?
  2. Bone structure: are your shoulders, hands and face angular and sharp, wide and blunt, moderate, or narrow and delicate?
  3. Flesh: is your body lean and straight, or soft and rounded, at any weight?
  4. Face: sharp, angular features, or rounded, soft ones?

Mostly long and sharp points to the Dramatic types. Wide and blunt points to the Naturals. Moderate and balanced points to the Classics. Compact with mixed features points to the Gamines. Soft and rounded points to the Romantics.

Common questions

Is Kibbe about body size?

No. Weight changes your flesh, not your bone structure or vertical line, so your type usually stays the same. Any type can be any size or height.

What's the difference between Kibbe and body shape (hourglass, pear and so on)?

Body shapes compare bust, waist and hip measurements. Kibbe looks at your whole line and bone structure, so two people with the same measurements can be different Kibbe types.

Can men use Kibbe?

Yes. The yin and yang lines apply to everyone, whatever you wear. A Dramatic man suits long, sharp tailoring just as a Dramatic woman does.
